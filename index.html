You want to look rested. A bit of color, as if you just got back from a week away.

Every method you've tried has a price. A sunbed costs you your skin. A bottle of self-tanner costs you your sheets and your palms. A salon costs you $50 and still leaves lines.

Here's what goes wrong with each one, and why it gets harder after 50.

The sunbed: color now, wrinkles later

The warnings you've heard are real.

In 2009, the World Health Organization's cancer agency moved tanning beds into its highest risk category, the same group as tobacco smoke.

After 50, your skin repairs itself more slowly and loses collagen faster. The UV that gives a 25-year-old a few fine lines cuts deeper grooves into your face.

You went for a healthy look and walked out with older skin.

Self-tanner: you never know what you'll get

You've tried them all. The expensive one from the department store, the cheap one from the drugstore, the mousses, the lotions, the gradual creams.

On mature skin, the result is a coin toss at best. You get orange palms you hide for days, dark rings around your ankles and patchy knees and elbows.

Worst of all, the color settles into the lines around your eyes, your mouth and your neck, and traces every one of them.

Then there's the smell, that sweet, biscuity odor that clings to your pillowcase. Your partner notices it before you're even in the room.

Spray tans: you pay more for the same problem

A salon sounds like the safe choice. Someone trained does it for you, with proper equipment.

But the spray uses the same active ingredient as the bottle at home, DHA. It lands on the surface of your skin and follows every contour.

On smooth young skin, that gives an even layer. On skin with fifty years behind it, the color pools in every crease.

You pay around $50 for that, and two weeks later you're booking the next one.

The sun: the oldest way does the most damage

Lying in the sun feels like the natural option.

UVA reaches deep into your skin and breaks down the elastin that keeps it firm. UVB burns the surface and leaves age spots behind.

Every unprotected hour speeds up wrinkles and thins out skin that is already getting thinner.

It's not you. It's how skin changes after 50

You didn't exfoliate wrong, and you didn't buy the wrong brand. After 50, your skin changes in three ways that make any color on the surface hard to get right:

One: cell turnover slows down. Dead cells stay on the surface longer and build up unevenly.
Two: your skin gets drier. Less natural oil means more texture and more fine cracks.
Three: collagen and elastin drop. Your skin folds more easily and lines get deeper.

Self-tanner works by reacting with that top layer of dead cells. Where the layer is thicker, the color comes out darker. Dry patches soak up more, and lines collect it.

That's why a tan that looks even on a 25-year-old turns patchy on you. It's chemistry, and a better bottle won't change it.

So what if the color didn't come from the surface at all?

Color from within

SunGlow™ is a tanning drop you drink. There's no mousse, lotion or spray involved.

You add a few drops to your morning coffee, tea or juice, and that's it. Nothing touches the surface of your skin.

How it works

SunGlow™ contains L-tyrosine and beta-carotene from carrot extract.

Your body uses L-tyrosine to make melanin, the same pigment that colors your skin in the sun. Beta-carotene settles in the skin and gives it a warm, golden tone.

Because the color comes from inside, there is nothing on the surface to streak, rub off on your towels or collect in your lines.

You don't apply anything, you don't wait for anything to dry, and nothing smells.
